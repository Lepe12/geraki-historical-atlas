PROSPECTING TRIP WAS 
STRENUOUS AND EXCITING 


Fred Hubler, who left last June 
with a party from Sexsmith to go 
prospecting on the Finlay River, ar- | 
rived home last Friday, after an en- | 
joyable though very strenuous sum-/ 
mer | 

After ‘the party had been there al 
couple of months they decided to! 


return home, but Fred elected to stay} 
a littl longer and do a 
pecting on his own. 

He brought home some very likely 
looking samples of quartz, some of 
them very liberally sprinkled with 


little pros-/} 


gold, some of which have been sent to | @pace. 


the Dominion assayer at Ottawa 

Fred claims that this one-man pros- 
pecting is not what it is cracked up 
to be. as on account of any serious 
accident one is more or less up against 
it He came across a number of 
parties washing gold on the river, with 
more or less success, 

Fred had several narrow escapes, 
the closest one being the time he met 
the Grizzly Bear. He was going 
through the bush one day when he 
came upon a grizzly suddenly. Fred 
felt like the coon who was in a like 
predicament and said, “Lord, if you 
can't help me, please don't help the 
bear—and you will see the damnedest 
fight you ever saw.” He fired several 
shots, and Mr. Bear took it on the run 
and went over a couple of rises, with 
Fred on his heels. Going through 
some heavy underbrush, following the 
trail of blood. Fred suddenly came 
upon bruin standing on his hind legs. 
The bear made a “Patsy O’Brien” pass 
at him, and just grazed his cheek, 
drawing blood. Fred decided then and 
there that this particular bear did not 
belong to him and made a very mas- 
terly retreat, fighting a rearguard 
action, somewhat after the famous re- 
treat from Mons. 

Mr. Hubler traded his pack pony for 
a boat at Fort Graham on the Finlay 
and drifted down as far as Dunvegan, 
catching a ride from there to Sex- 
smith, 

If the report from Ottawa is en- 
couraging, Fred intends to make the 
trip again next summer.
