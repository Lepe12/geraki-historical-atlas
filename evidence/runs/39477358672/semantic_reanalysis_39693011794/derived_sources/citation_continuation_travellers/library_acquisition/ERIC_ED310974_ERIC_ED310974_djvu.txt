ABSTRACT 

Social historians are taught that historical changes 
are caused by large social and economic forces, rather than great 
individuals; and so they study groups of people, broad economic 
movements, and underlying institutional structures that change slowly 
over time. But the general public demands information about the 
individual person told through historical narrative. Through this 
method, historians can still raise important and stimulating 
questions of broad significance. By studying Christopher Columbus as
