 The island of Alonnesos mentioned hère by Strabo was not the modem Alonnesos, but one of the two islands to the north, Pelagonisi or Agios Eustratios.7 Its only historical significance is that in the 5th c. B.C. it was part of the Athenian empire, and in 345 B.C., was seized by a pirate named Sostratus. Philip of Macedon freed the island from the pirates, but kept it to himself.8 In 343, the Athenians demanded its return, and when Philip refused Demosthenes delivered a furious speech against him.9 As a resuit, the Peparethians seized Alonnesos, and threw out the Macedonian garrison. Philip in return attacked the island of Peparethus and took it,10 thus renewing the hostilities with Athens, who prepared for war. Thèse actions indicate both Philip s imperialistic ambitions, as well as the stratégie importance of the islands.
