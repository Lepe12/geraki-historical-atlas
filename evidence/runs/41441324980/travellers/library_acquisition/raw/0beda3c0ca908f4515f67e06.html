Suggestions for revision or reasons for rejection
The manuscript is much improved. Most of the deficiencies in the interpretation of the data have been corrected. However, in revising the Discussion, the authors have introduced a new topic (magma sources) that was not dealt with in the previous version, is not supported by any data presented and relies totally on analogy with another study (Popa et al., 2020). This text (section 4.3.3) should be removed from the Discussion. Also, the authors retain review in the Discussion (section 4.1) that ought to be removed or else shifted to the Introduction and shortened. Finally, in the Abstract and Introduction, the authors suggest that the submarine versus setting as part of the selling point but fail to discuss this aspect further. It is not considered in the Discussion.

I have noted queries and made corrections to the pdf version of the manuscript.

ED: Publish subject to minor revisions (further review by editor) (25 Mar 2021) by Peter Abbott
Dear Xiaolong Zhou

Many thanks for submitting the revised version of your manuscript and addressing the comments of myself and Prof. McPhie. I note your response regarding Section 4.1, but do agree with Prof. McPhie that aspects of this section would be more appropriate in the Introduction, specifically Lines 466-482. This part of Section 4.1 provides a useful description of the disadvantages of the K-Ar method versus the advantages of the Ar-Ar method, which I feel is a more general review and not a specific discussion of your data. This section provides a justification for your study and the Ar-Ar approach taken so would work well after Section 1.2 where you describe the past studies that exploited K-Ar dating and you can justify the need to revisit the geochronology of Milos using Ar-Ar dating and introduce the advantages of the technique. The second part of Section 4.1 does provide a discussion of your data so is appropriate for the Discussion section so I don't think that requires moving or revising.

So I would request that you further consider moving the first part of Section 4.1 into your introduction, which I feel is a minor change and would help to provide further justification for your study and the production of the new dates.

Best Wishes
Peter

Short summary
High-resolution geochronology is one of the key factors to predict volcanic eruptions. To build up a high-resolution geochronological framework, we reported 21 new high-precision eruption ages (40Ar / 39Ar) for a ~ 3.3 × 106-year-old volcanic field: Milos (Greece). In combination with geochemical information and eruption volumes from the volcanoes of Milos, the long-lived volcanic history could provide important clues for the prediction of volcanic eruptions.
