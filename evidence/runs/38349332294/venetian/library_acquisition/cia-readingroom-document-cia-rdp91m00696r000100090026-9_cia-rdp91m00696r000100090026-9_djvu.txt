13 July 1976 


MEMORANDUM FOR THE RECORD 
SUBJECT: Coordination Group Meeting, July 12, 1976 


1. The DDCI is determined to have for the Senate Select 
Committee on Intelligence (SSCI), when it reconvenes next week, 
answers to the follow-up questions from the Agency's June 16 
briefing on the Committee and from Mr. Knoche's confirmation 
hearing on 25 June. The Legislative Counsel's Office is gather- 
ing these answers, and the full package will be circulated to 
all group members later this week. 


2. Legislative Counsel also has a raft of questions from 
Senator Hart. They apparently rephrase the recommendations of 
the Select Committee into questions that ask what the Agency is 
doing about the recommendations. The only one I noted that 
affected us concerned whether academicians must inform their in- 
stitutions of their relations with the Agency. The group was of 
the opinion that the answer was negative, but there might still 
be a tactical question of what one said to Senator Hart at this 
time. 


3. Finally, the Assistant Legislative Counsel circulated 
a memo for the record (copy attached) which contained a list of 
commitments the DDCI had made to the SSCI during his confirmation 
hearing. We were all asked to review these commitments with a 
view to a later discussion of which we would wish to retreat from.

MEMORANDUM FOR THE RECORD 


SUBJECT: Confirmation Hearing of E. Henry Knoche to be 
Deputy Director of Central Intelligence, 23 June 1976 


1. At 9:45 a.m. on 23 June 1976, the Senate Select Committee on 
Intelligence met in open session on the nomination of E. Henry .Knoche to 
be Deputy Director of Central Intelligence. The hearing was held in 
Room 235, Russell Senate Office Building. A complete transcript of the 
hearing was taken by Ward and Paul Reporting Company. 


2. Present from the Committee were: 


Senator Daniel Inouye (D., Hawaii), Chairman

Senator Robert Morgan (D., N.C.)

5. Chairman Inouye opened the session by stating that the Committee 
had heard from Mr. Knoche in executive session the previous week and that 
Mr. Knoche demonstrated that "his qualities for the post are of an extraordinarily 
high order." The Chairman viewed the nomination of Mr. Knoche as an 
important indication of the increasing emphasis the U.S. Government places 
on the intelligence analysis function. During the course of the hearing, 
Senators Baker, Thurmond, Bayh, Garn, Stevenson, and Morgan all spoke 
highly of Mr. Knoche's qualifications and announced their intention to 
support his nomination, 


6. After the opening remarks by Chairman Inouye and Senator Baker, 
Mr. Knoche read a brief opening statement. All members of the Committee 
then questioned Mr. Knoche. In response to questions, Mr. Knoche: 
a, Aseced to provide the Committee a full set of CIA 
regulations and notify them of changes; 


b. Recommended providing NSCID's to Committee, but 
said NSC is the custodian; 


c. Agreed to get together with GAO officials, work up 
suggested guidelines for GAO audits, and submit them to the Committee; 


d. Agreed to work with the staff to develop pro- 
cedures to report all oS projects to the Committee, not just 
the major ones} . 


e. Agreed to instruct the Inspector General and the 
General Counsel to submit to the Committee reports similar 
to those provided the IOB; 


f. Agreed to instruct the General Counsel to notify the 
Committee when he refers allegations that activities by CIA 
employees violate Federal law to the Department of Justice; 


g. Agreed to regularly remind CIA employees to bring 
to top management's attention activities which raise questions of 
legality or propriety; 


h. Acknowledged that CIA employees should be able to 
come directly to the Committee if they are fearful-—for whatever 
reason--of reporting a questionable activity to the Inspector 
General or IOB; 


i. Agreed to work with the Committee to establish ground 
rules to keep the Committee informed of Inspector General surveys; 


j. Agreed to volunteer substantive information to Congress 
that is relevant to the broad spectrum of Committee's interests.
