                    Culture
                    
History & heritage
                    

                        

                            
Kimolos has been inhabited since prehistoric times and gave its name to kimolia (chalk) — the white, absorbent rock (Kimolos earth) mined here since antiquity and exported throughout the Mediterranean, used in cleaning, dyeing and medicine. The island was one of the first outposts of the Cycladic Civilization at the southwestern edge of the archipelago.
In antiquity, the city of Kimolos stood at what is now Ellinika, on the western coast, where the sunken ruins and extensive necropolises can be seen today. The island minted its own coinage and was closely linked with neighboring Milos. Geological changes gradually submerged a large part of the ancient city into the sea.
In medieval times, Kimolos passed, along with the rest of the Cyclades, to the Venetian Duchy of the Aegean. Relentless pirate raids forced the inhabitants to fortify themselves: first at Paliokastro inland, and later at the Kastro of today's Chora, with the wall-houses forming a defensive ring. Ottoman rule followed, with the island maintaining a maritime and agricultural life.
After incorporation into the Greek state in the 19th century, Kimolos's economy relied on fishing, livestock farming and, above all, mineral mining — kimolia (chalk), bentonite and perlite. In recent decades, the island has deliberately kept its quiet, authentic character unchanged, promoting soft tourism as an alternative to the mass development of larger destinations.

                    Explore
                    
Sights & Trails
                    
Monasteries, castles, ancient towns and hiking routes.                    

                        

                                                            
                                    Kastro
                                    

The medieval fortified settlement of Chora, divided into Mesa and Exo Kastro. The outer houses form a continuous wall for protection against pirates, while inside, old churches and the Church of Christ from 1592 survive.                                
                                                            
                                    Skiadi
                                    

The striking geological formation shaped like a mushroom or umbrella, in the island's western part. It was created by the differential erosion of volcanic rocks — white and dark — and is Kimolos's most recognizable symbol.                                
                                                            
                                    Paliokastro
                                    

The ruins of the medieval capital, atop a hill in the island's interior. The fortified settlement was destroyed by a pirate raid, leading the inhabitants to build today's Chora.                                
                                                            
                                    Ellinika
                                    

On the western coast, on the isthmus opposite the islet of Agios Andreas, lie the sunken ruins of the ancient city of Kimolos — walls, tombs and building remains lost to the sea through geological changes.                                
                                                            
                                    Polyaigos
                                    

The largest uninhabited island in the Aegean, facing Kimolos. A protected Natura 2000 area, with wild goats, imposing rocks and turquoise coves — accessible only by boat.                                
                                                            
                                    Archaeological Museum
                                    

In Chora, it houses geometric, archaic and classical vessels, figurines and finds from Ellinika and the island's necropolises, illuminating Kimolos's long history.                                
                                                            
                                    Folklore & Maritime Museum
                                    

Housed in Chora, it presents traditional daily life, maritime tradition and the trades of the people of Kimolos through objects, tools and photographs.                                
                                                            
                                    Monastery of Panagia Odigitria
                                    

A historic church in Chora, one of the island's most important religious monuments, safeguarding old icons and ecclesiastical treasures.                                
                                                            
                                    Hot Springs (Prassa)
                                    

Near Prassa beach, hot mineral waters bubble up — evidence of the island's volcanic nature, known since antiquity for their therapeutic properties.                                
                                                            
                                    Klima & Goupa
                                    

The two fishing villages with the "syrmata", colorful boat garages carved into the rock for storing boats — among the most picturesque spots on the Kimolos coastline.                                
                                                            
                                    Church of Christ
                                    

The historic Church of Christ inside the Kastro of Chora, built in 1592 — one of the oldest and most important monuments on Kimolos.                                
                                                            
                                    Church of Agios Ioannis Chrysostomos
                                    

The parish church of Chora, dedicated to Saint John Chrysostom, with a remarkable carved wooden iconostasis and old icons.

Local flavour
                
                
WELCOME! This is how you will be greeted on the island known as Kimolis, Kimoulo, Kimilos, but also Echinousa or Ophiousa. The Venetians named it Argentiera or Arzenteria, meaning silver land, and they influenced its gastronomic identity more than any other conqueror. A barren and small place, repeatedly burned down throughout its history by raids, it does not boast a vast variety of products. However, it has an interesting cheese-making tradition, unique herbs and spices, aromatic vegetables, and the tastiest and most famous pies of the Cyclades, with the star being the ladénia.

                    Getting here                    
Access & transport                                    
                                        

            

                
 How to get there
                
Kimolos has no airport. The port of Psathi is connected by ferry to Piraeus (about 4–8 hours, depending on the route) and to the other islands on the line, while a small daily ferry/boat connects it with neighboring Milos — the easiest way to get there.
            
            

                
 Getting around the island
                
The island is small: a local bus connects Psathi with Chora and the main southern beaches, while for the more remote beaches, Skiadi and the dirt roads, renting a scooter or car is the most convenient option. Many wild spots and Polyaigos are reached by boat.
