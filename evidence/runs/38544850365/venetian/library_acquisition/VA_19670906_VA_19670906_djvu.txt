Expo Is Fascinating Fun-filled Week 


(In an earlier issue the summer 
trip to Expo by County students 
was described by Larry Mumby. 
This is the concluding portion of 
this account.) 

The County of Vulcan Centen¬ 
nial Travellers spent five days at 
the Expo site. Perhaps the easiest 
way to describe Expo Is to give 
a quick tour of the three islands. 

Each day, the tourist began by 
a bus trip from Elmville Student 
Village to Expo. A short walk 
from the parking lot brought the 


tourist to Place d’Accueil where 
he had his passport stamped allow¬ 
ing his admission to Expo. 

From Place d'Accueil he could 
tour the Expo Theatre, the CBC 
Pavilion as well as enjoy the 
beauty of the Industrial Design, 
Fine Arts, and Photography 
Pavilions. 

The tourist could then descend 
the steps at Place d’Accueil and 
'board the Expo Express. The Expo 
Express was a fully remote con¬ 
trol train donated by the Swiss 


government .to Expo. A ride upon 
this train gives a complete view 
of Expo. 

As the train leaves Place 
d’Accueil, the tourist sees, on the 
left, the Expo Harbour, where 
vessels such as the Bluenose are 
moored for inspection by tourists. 
On the right, he passes the im¬ 
mense architectural wonder of 
Habitat ’67. These marvellous 
apartments (158 in all) were fur¬ 
nished by Good Housekeeping and 
Chatelaine. The rent, unfurnished, 


is estimated between $500 and 
$700 per month. 

Next, the tourist sees the pavi¬ 
lion of Man and His Health and 
the pavilion of Man and His Com¬ 
munity. Within these pavilions the 
tourist sees man’s control over 
his problems in health, as well as 
a complete history of the medical 
field. He also views the different 
characteristics-..of man — laziness, 
segregation, destruction, urbanism 
and conqueror..

Fire Department 
Aids In Brant 
Farm Blaze 

The rural pumper of the Vul¬ 
can Fire Department was called 

out to the Brant district at noon 
Tuesday of this week to a fire 
which destroyed a bunk house and 
two other machinery or equipment 
sheds at the Stacy Graham farm. 
The farm is located in sight of 
Highway 23 about four miles 
north-east of Brant. 

A moderate wind which blew 
burning garbage from the barrel 
against the bunkhouse, started the 
fire which also burned a stack of 
bales and 15 acres of swathed 
grain. It was feared at one time 
that the south wind might blow 
the fire through the quarter- 
section to the highway.

gathered one night recently to in¬

Susamma Varghese, left front, 
and Mariamma Varghese are two 
cousins from New Delhi, India, 
who are entering the nursing field 
in this part of the world. On Aug. 
5, they arrived by air at Montreal 
and two days later they were 
staring from the local nurses’ 
quarters at miles and miles of 
flat green farm land. 

The girls trained at Guntur and 


their home is at Punalur-Kevala 
and spent four years nursing at 
New Delhi. It was through friends 
at Calgary and Edmonton that 
they heard of employment oppor« 
tunity at Vulcan. 

English was taught at school in 
India but the rapid and flavored 
speech of the girls is as yet 
somewhat hazy to the listener. At 
the hospital they are with other 
girls constantly to eliminate any 
language problems. 

By now the K‘ r W iave probably 
taken on our* western attire but 
at the time their street clothes 


were saris, common apparel to 
their native country. 

You could imagine a patient 
waking to find either of the pert 
and pretty nurses in the back¬ 
ground, Alice Mathewson and 
Donna Jenks taking his pulse and 
wondering, “What’re these gals up 
to now". But to come out of deep 
sleep and be holding hands with 
Susamma or Mariamma ... a 
fellow might envision the glorious 
splendour of the Taj Mahal, the 
maidens and the wafting fans and 
all. Or he might suspect he’s a 
lot sicker Utah the doctor told 
him he was.

BIG CUCUMBER 

The biggest cucumber we’ve 
seen in many years was brought 
into The Advocate office last 
week by Dave Decker of the 
Newdale Hutterite Colony at 
Queenstown. 

Solid and perfectly formed, the 
big cuke weighed almost three 
pounds and was grown in the 
colony's big irrigated garden.

On Monday morning your cor¬

He asked if It were possible to put 
this item in the paper so that all 
residents might read it and give 
it a bit of thought: He said we 
have a fine little village, lovely 
sidewalks, many nice homes and 
buildings, trees, etc., however in 
strolling around one must walk 
on the street as the trees have 
such low branches that it is im¬ 
possible to walk under many of 
them. Also people’s hedges are 
sorely in need of trimming. One 
has to step off the sidewalk to 
get t around them. He said it is 
quite possible that the people 
themselves are so accustomed to 
this that they take no notice of 
it; and that perhaps if their at¬ 
tention were drawn to it, they 
would take more pride in it and 
try to change these conditions; 
making the village look as though 
it were lived in rather than a 
ghost town that is growing up in

FAT STOCK SHOW 

Paul Maier, a local Shorthorn 
breeder, has completed quite a 
successful summer on the show 
circuit. Even though he did not 
show his regular line of short¬ 
horns, he presented keen compe¬ 


tition to others in the market 
-glasses with steers. 

At the Lethbridge Exhibition, 
Mr. Maier’s Shorthorn steer placed 
second in the carcass class. In the 
live judging, it stood only fourth 
receiving 18 out of 28 points. How¬ 
ever G3 out of 72 points were 
picked up on the carcass appraisal 
for a lot of 81 of a possible 100. 
The animal yielded 61.8# — high¬ 
est 'of the class. The first place 
steer, a Hereford, shown by Steve * 
Balog of Milk River under Judge 
John Hay of Nanton was given 
26 points of 28 in live placing and 
an additional 57 points out of 72 
awarded in carcass appraisal for 
a total of 83 of a possible 100. The 
third place animal, a Hereford, 
shown by McIntyre Ranching Co. 
was given the full 28 out of 28 
points possible for “live” judging, 
scored only 33 points out of 72 in 
carcass appraisal with a final 
score of 61. 

The second shoiw that of Medi¬ 
cine Hat Exhibition brought in 
first place prize money to Mr. 
Maier. The Shorthorn steer placed 
in first position in live judging 
and remained first spot in carcass 
grading also. Mr. Lore of Edmon¬ 
ton was the keen judge. 

The Champion steer at Vauxhall 
Fair recently was also exhibited 
by Mr. Maier. This animal was a 
cross-bred and the class was only 
judged by live placings.
