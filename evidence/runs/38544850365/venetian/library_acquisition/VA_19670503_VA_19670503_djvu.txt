ELK ISLAND 
NATIONAL PARK 
HOME OF THE 
BUFFALO! 



One of North America’s few 
remaining buffalo herds 
can be found 30 miles east 
of Edmonton in Elk Island 
National Park. It is 
estimated that now there 
are more than ten thousand 
of these great beasts in the 
park. This is a surprising 
figure, considering that the 
last wild survivors — a 
group of eleven — were 
all that remained in Alberta 
in 1880. 

The herd at Elk Island is 
reputed to be the finest of 
its type in the world. 

Yearly, thousands of tourists 
visit the park in hope of 
catching a glimpse 
of the herd. 

It’s another 

"feather-in-the-cap for our 
province — that Albertans 
saw the danger of 
extinction and took steps to 
preserve the buffalo. All 
Albertans can be proud 
that Alberta is “home” to 
thousands of buffalo that 
might never have been.

New Alberta Farm Guide 
Valuable To Every Farmer 


A book on all phases of Alberta 
agriculture — the Centennial ed¬ 
ition of the Alberta Farm Guide— 
is available to farmers and agri¬ 
cultural advisors, Alriculture Min¬ 
ister Harry E. Strom said recently. 

He said the information in the 
guide, if aipplied, could Increase in¬ 
come on each Alberta farm by 
thousands of dollars yearly. This 
would result in a better level of 
living for farmers and better-qual¬ 
ity food for all Albertans. 

The 240 pages of this centennial 
edition were prepared by 68 leading 
agricultural specialists from the 
Faculty of Agriculture, University 
of Alberta* the Research Branch, 
Canada Department of Agriculture 
and the Alberta Department of 
Agriculture. 

The care andNjusbandry of farm 
animals as small as the bumble bee 
and as large as \the 2,000-pound 
bull are related in) the guide. Sec¬ 
tions deal with agricultural econo¬ 
mics, agricultural engineering, bee¬ 
keeping, climate, I cereals, plant 
diseases and pests, weed control, 
forage crops, cultural practices and 
crop sequences, horticulture, irriga¬ 
tio n, l i v estock h rcrc Hn g, beef cattle, 
dairy cattle, swine, sheep, and 
poultry. In addition there are sec¬ 
tions on livestock diseases and 
pests, soil fertility and zonation, 
soil management and agricultural 
services. A new section in the book 
deals with fur farming and there 
is a special agricultural appendix 
dealing with matters such as 
weights and measures tables. 

“The purpose of publishing the 
Alberta Farm Guide every three 
years,” Mr. Strom said, "is to keep 
farmers and agricultural advisors 
up to dale on production develop¬ 
ments in agriculture. With an es¬ 
timated 150 agricultural scientists 
working in Alberta alone, there is 
a discovery of importance to far¬ 
mers happening in the province al¬ 


most every week. All these scienti¬ 
fic findings are translated Into 
practical farm practices and incor¬ 
porated into the Alberta Farm 
Guide.” 

The first Alberta Farm Guide 
was published in 1959 at the re¬ 
quest of the Alberta Institute or 
Agrologists who wanted provincial 
fanners to have a handbook. This 
book has won favor with district 
agriculturists, technical advisors in 
agri-business and teachers, as well 
as farmers. 

A farmer interested in starting a 
new enterprise can get the main 
production information he needs 
from the guide. The Alberta Farm 
Guide, although it deals with the 
main points in every topic, does 
not deal exhaustively with every¬ 
one. But a reading list for more 
information on specialized subjects 
is available at the end of every 
section in the guide. 

The Alberta Farm Guide was 
specifically designed for farmers 
and agrologists in search of infor¬ 
mation in a hurry. Each section 
deals with a specific subject. Each 
section is divided into sub-sections 
itt cumpiotuiy cover me subject. 
There is cross-referencing to sub¬ 
jects related to the section being 
read can be found in a hurry. 

The guide is not an ivory-tower 
textbook, but a practical, down-to- 
earth book on farming with de¬ 
finite recommendations and specific 
figures. All production and business 
problems of Alberta farming are 
dealt with here. 

Finally, the Alberta Farm Guide 
is easy to read. It contains familiar 
words, short sentences and short 
paragraphs which get directly to 
the point. 

The Alberta Farm Guide is avail¬ 
able from district agriculturists 
throughout Alberta and from the 
Publications Branch, Alberta De¬ 
partment of Agriculture, Edmonton.

A. V. Symes 
Dies At Calgary 

Arthur Vivian Symes passed 
away at the Colonel Belcher Hos¬ 
pital in Calgary on Thursday, 
April 27, 1967 at the age of 86. 

He was born in Clifton, England, 
on July 4, 1880. In 1899, he en¬ 
listed and served in the South 
African Boer War with the British 
Forces, 8th Division, later trans¬ 
ferring to the Mounted Constabu¬ 
lary. After this, he re-enlisted and 
served with the British Imperial 
Army in various parts of the 
world. 

He came to Canada in 1909 and 
worked In the Millarville, Glelchen 
and Queenstown area until 1914. 
He enlisted in August and served 
in World War I as a bombardier 
in the British Royal Artillery. Fol¬ 
lowing the Armistice, he returned 
to Canada and farmed in the 
Queenstown district. Later he 
worked at the Buffalo Hills Ranch 
for a number of years. He also 
lived at the Vulcan Legion Hall 
for some time before moving to 
Calgary in 1963. He was an origi¬ 
nal member of the Wheat Pool 
and a life member of the Cana¬ 
dian Legion. 

Known relatives surviving are 
two nieces, Mrs. Mary Hutchings, 
Somerset, England, and Mrs. 
Vivian Aldridge, Bristol, England. 

Interment was in the Field of 
Honor, Burns land Cemetery, 
Calgary.

The 

Recreation Area 
Vote... 

On May 16 residents of the part of the county within the borders 
of the proposed Vulcan Recreation Area will be asked to vote on By-law 
No. 603, in favor or against the formation of the Recreation Area. Vulcan 
townspeople are not affected by this as Town Council has already given 
third reading to this by-law favoring an agreement with the county for 
establishment of this recreation area. 

In an effort to assist voters in clearly understanding the issue in 
the by-law the following facts should be made clear: 

The Vulcan and District Recreation Centre and the proposed Vulcan 
Recreation Area are two separate entities. The former has been in existence 
for several years and its only purpose is to build and help maintain a com¬ 
munity centre-recreation complex over future years for Vulcan and district. 

Although an amendment to the Cultural Development Act states that 
money may be expended “for capital works related to a recreation program 
within the municipality or to a joint recreation program,” there is no thought 

-by t h e V ulca n . Dist r ict R au r oatio n C e ntr a- c a mm i tt ao th at a ny or all—money- 

collected by the Vulcan Recreation Area be channelled into the construction 
of the proposed Vulcan recreation centre building. It is however, hoped that 
the administration committee of the Vulcan Recreation Area will see fit to 
assist in yearly overhead and maintenance costs of such a building or buildings. 

The Vulcan and District Recreation Centre has been instrumental in pro¬ 
moting establishment of a recreation area for this district. Following the policy 
advocated by the Provincial Government Recreation Branch, Lomond, Cham¬ 
pion and Milo already have recreation areas. 

If this by-law is passed, a levy of one mill for town and rural pro¬ 
perty owners would be assessed annually and this money would be administered 
by a seven member board. On this board would be four representatives appointed 
by the County Council and three members appointed by Town Council. It is 
likely that the Recreation Board would be composed of one member of the 
town council and two other town residents recommended to the town council for 
appointment to the Board, and one member of the county council and three 
other residents of the rural part of the recreation area who would be appointed 
to the Board by the County Council. The seven-member Board would probably 
include one or two women members. All requests for annual financial assistance 
from organizations concerned with recreation would be submitted to this 
committee. 

Money collected would be used to help organizations develop and encour¬ 
age recreation programs within the area and to help meet overhead costs of 
eligible groups. 

Establishment of a Vulcan Recreation Area will provide the machinery 
for governmental assistance both financial and through help of appointed recrea¬ 
tion officers presently stationed in key centres of the province. 

In the future it is possible to envisage a full time recreation director 
Being located at Vulcan to serve the area and through development of this 
recreation theme, part of his salary is paid by the provincial government. This 
is only one example of such possible assistance. 

In summary, approval of the by-law would mean properly or¬ 
ganized recreation programs in this area, better financing and close 
supervision of all funds expended on recreation. In the interest of the 
betterment of the town and district we ask your support in this by-law.

SNOW MOLD ON LAWNS 

If you have snow mold on your 
lawn this spring, you would be 
wise to slop it from spreading by 
sweeping the greyish web-like 
growth off the grass with a stiff 
broom. A metal rake is not re¬ 
commended because it could injure 
the grass roots. 

According to P. D. McCalla, 

Supervisor of Horticulture with the 
Alberta Plant Industry Division, 
snow mold is by far the most 
common cause of winter injury to 
grass in Alberta. It may appear on 
the turf in the fall, winter or 
spring. 

When dry weather checks the 
fungus growth, injury to the grass 
may be only superficial, but even 
superficial damage can weaken 
grass growth to the point where 
weed encroachment is encouraged. 

Often the fungus penetrates the 
turf deeply enough to either kill 
or severely injure it. 

Mr. McCalla suggests that any¬ 
one who has snow mold on his 
lawn this spring might be wise to 
treat the turf towards the end of 
October for protection the follow¬ 
ing year. He recommends using a 
2:1 mixture of Calomel and cor¬ 
rosive sublimite at a rate of $ to 4
