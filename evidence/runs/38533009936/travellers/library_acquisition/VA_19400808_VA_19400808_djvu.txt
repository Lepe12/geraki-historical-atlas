Finger Printing Canada 

The idea of having everyone in Can¬ 
ada over 16 years of age register is a 
plendid one and will meet with the 
approval of every loyal citizen. But 
the government should go farther than 
mere registration. While it is under¬ 
taking the task everyone who regis¬ 
ters should be compelled to give his or 
her fingerprints. Even- before the war 
:t was the opinion of most people that 
there should be a method of regis¬ 
tering finger-prints. Now the means 
and the method are at hand and the 
government shouldn’t overlook this 
golden opportunity to get complete 
dentification of every person in Can¬ 
ada. The records would be invaluable 
for army and civic purposes and by 
making it part of the registration it 
could be done without any extra labor 
or expense.—Amherstburg Echo,

Paying For Planes 

The women of Canada deserve all 
.he credit possible for the various 
wbemes they have undertaken to pur¬ 
chase training planes and other types 
of. aircraft for the war effort. But in 
my opinion there is some confusion in 
the public mind about what is being at¬ 
tempted and accomplished in these 
drives. Their net effect is to help the 
Minister of Finance and not the De¬ 
partment of Defence.They are schemes 
for helping to pay for the war other¬ 
wise than by taxes or loans. They will 
not result in getting one man or one 
plane to the front more quickly than 
would otherwise be the case. Our air 
defence forces are now limited in their 
expansion only by the physical capac- 
.ty of North American pfants to pro¬ 
duce planes, and by the ability of the 
I training schools to turn out pilots. 

Not long ago 1 saw that the city of 
Victoria had donated ten thousand 
dollars to help buy a bomber. The net 
effect of that decision was to make the 
city pay for what the Dominion treas¬ 
ury would otherwise have had to de¬ 
fray. The national treasury is the on¬ 
ly public treasury in the best position 
to levy taxes or borrow money' for 
war purposes. We will probably all end 
up.better off if we all stick to our re¬ 
spective fields.Elmore Philpotts in 
Vernon News.

Q UITE a number of westerners will see reason in 
the opinion of W. F. Itickart, M.P., for Durham, 
Ontario, when he said recently in the House that the 
producer should not demand guaranteed price on more 
than 5000 bushels of wheat in any one crop year. Mr. 
Rickart said: “The Ontario farmers are willing to help 
needy western farmers, but huge mechanized farms can 
deliver up to 60,000 bushels a year and 1 do not believe 
th'ey are entitled to the full protection of guaranteed 
price.” • ~ ’ 

Local farmers have from time to time expressed some¬ 
thing the same thought. One viewpoint frequently heard 
is that the guaranteed price should be better than 70 
cents, but on smaller bushelage per farmer. A guaranteed 
initial priee of $1:00 on 2500 bushels from any one farm, 
the balance of output to go on world market, would have 
much more widespread benefit. It is possible that the 
cost might not be any more to the country at large than 
a guaranteed minimum over all. . 

No one knows better than westerners that the trend 
'toward large scale farming is destructive to rural settle¬ 
ment. The small farmer is absolutely dependent on a 
reasonable price for grain. His continuance on his quar¬ 
ter or. half section is possible only if a fair price is main¬ 
tained. But if his returns are such as to give him a de¬ 
cent livelihood, it will mean permanent rural settlement, 
families growing up, more purchasing power which will 
affect all industry. It is incomparably better for the 
community, for the west and for all Canada, to have ten

REMEMBER that no outsider is going to help you 
build your town. Every time you give business to an 
outsider that can be as advantageously placed with 
your home town firms, you are making the com¬ 
munity poorer. 

It is the local business houses who pay 
wages to local people, who pay the 
heaviest taxes, who are called on to sub¬ 
scribe to the various organizations and 
appeals lor help. 

WE ADMIRE the customer who gives the home 
towner an even break. There are many little serv¬ 
ices and accomodations given that no outsider can 
or will give. Be loyal to your own community—for 
one good turn deserves another.

of growth at first, but it may be usq)
