U.S. Farmers Take Thought 

Farmers in the United States appear to be 
seriously concerned about that country’s policies of 
dealing with agricultural surpluses. They are gra¬ 
dually discovering what the non-farming consumers 
knew long ago, that support prices, soil banks, 
government controls and direction are not satis¬ 
factory substitutes for the free market. 

Naturally, there are several plans for dealing 
with the farmers’ problems, and all may not be work¬ 
able. The newsworthy development is that the plans 
are now being advanced by the farmers’ own organi¬ 
zations and not by the federal agricultural authorities. 
The* main purpose of the new plans is to get rid of 
the surpluses of major crops, in which some nine bil¬ 
lion dollars of taxpayers’ money is now tied up, and 
thus get back to prices set by the old-fashioned 
method of supply and demand. 

No group of farmers has yet proposed getting 
along entirely without government intervention, but 
it is possible that, if they find they can solve some 
of their problems themselves, they will be encourag¬ 
ed to press for more freedom and finally to remove 
the burden of agricultural surpluses from the 
shoulders of the general taxpayer. 

At the moment, the farmers seem to contemplate 
a program of first disposing of the overhanging sur¬ 
pluses and then limiting future production to what 
their markets will conveniently absorb. They still look 
for government enforcement of the second part of 
the program, but it might be that if their final ob¬ 
jective of better incomes were attained, they might 
find that they could get the government out of the 
farming business altogether. 

Canadian farmers will watch the new United 
States developments with interest, for the surpluses 
in that country have a bearing on agricultural pros¬ 
perity here. If they can see farm problems south of 
the border solved by the farmers’ own action, they 
may be encouraged to take thought about similar 
solutions for similar Canadian problems.

Letters to the Editor 


To Whom It May Concern: 

We are writing in the interests 
of parents, who would like very 
much to organize a Home and 
School Association If co-operation 
can be attained. Such an organiza¬ 
tion is needed in this area in order 
to give the public an opportunity 
to voice opinions in school affairs. 

For example, (a) the school 
auditoriums have cost the public 
a good deal to build and yet when 
a choral workshop group, sponsor¬ 
ed by the Department of Educa¬ 
tion culminated their activities in 
a program, they were charged $25 
for the use of the auditorium. A 
Home and School Association, had 
it been in existence, could have 
sponsored this program and no ad¬ 
mission fee would have been neces¬ 
sary. The Choral Workshop pro¬ 
gram was not an effort to make 
money and quite possibly the $25 
collected has been or will be used 
for a good purpose but we are 
wondering what that purpose is. 

(b) The auditoriums have a 
stage. Why can these not be put 
to use? The schools in themselves 
do not present Christmas or other 
programs anymore, perhaps the 
parents and teachers could bring 
back this once-popular custom. 
One concert, taking in all children, 
would not be necessary or possible 
perhaps, but afternoon or evening 
programs for an individual grade 
or grades could be arranged. We 
would like to see what our child¬ 
ren are learning in music, drama¬ 
tics, memory work, etc. 

These are only two of the many 
reasons why an organization such 
as Home and School would be 
beneficial. However, we need co¬ 
operation of other parents and the 
teachers. Do we have It? 

—Mr. and Mrs. Evan Williams

New 2 Storey $500,000 Aquarium 
Being Constructed in Calgary 


Construction has started on a 
$500,000 aquarium and required 
equipment which is being built • by 
the Calgary Brewing and Malting 
Company Ltd. pt the company’s 
plant in East Calgary. The aquarium 
will be one of tne largest in North 
America. 

President of Calgary Brewing 
and Malting, J. B. Cross, said the 
aquarium is the first stage in a plan 
which in later years will include 
construction of a large, western- 
theme museum adjoining the 
aquarium. 

“Both the museum and the 
aquarium are planned to provide 
Albertans with an interesting and 
educational visiting place, and as 
attractions which will be of interest 
to tourists,” Mr. Cross said. 

The two-storey reinforced con¬ 
crete and steel building—140 by 65 
feet in area—was designed by J. 
Stevenson and Associates and is 
being constructed by Poole Con 
struction Co. Ltd. Basement will, 
house mechanical equipment, while 
the main floor will contain 40 small 
tanks for tropical fish, 24 tanks for 
larger species of fresh and salt 
water fish, and a pit for alligators 
and other reptile exhibits. The 
aquarium will be open to the public 


by next August, Mr. Cross said It 
will adjoin the present fish 
hatcheries building, but will in no 
way affect the trout-rearing oper¬ 
ation now being carried on there 
Rather, the new project will be 
complementary to the fish hatchery 
and the beautiful rockeries and fish 
ponds which are attractions of the 
Brewery gardens. 

The second floor of the new 
aquarium will be used to display 
Western historical e v hibits until the 
museum adjoining me aquarium is 
constructed. The museum is ten¬ 
tatively planned as a twO-storey 
circular structure 180 feet in 
diameter which will be joined to 
the aquarium at ground level 
by an open patio and by a cross¬ 
walk at the second storey. 

Museum material has been col¬ 
lected by the Brewery for several 
years and research is continuing so 
that, on completion, the museum 
will reflect the history of the 
Western horseman from the days of 
Cortez to the present. Lifelike 
models, including those of such 
famous westerners as Chief Crow¬ 
foot, Col. Macleod, Pete Knight, 
.’ohn Ware, and portrayals of 
specific historic incidents in Alberta 
are planned

VILLAGE OF LOMOND 

SYNOPSIS OF BY-LAW NO. 161 

This By-law provides for the issuance of debentures of 
the Village in the sum of $79,400.00, for the purpose of pro¬ 
viding a water supply and distribution system for the Village. 

The By-law authorizes the Council to enter into con¬ 
tracts for the installation of the said system, and for such pur¬ 
pose to issue debentures of the Village in the sum of $79,400.00. 

The sum of $45,462.00 is to be charged by way of special 
assessment for local improvements against all properties 
abutting the streets or avenues wherein the said water system 
is to be installed, payable at an annual rate per front foot of 
frontage of 47 cents, payable for a period of Twenty (20) 
years. The balance is borrowed on the credit of the Village at 
large and provision is made for the levying of annual rates on 
all taxable property In the Village to provide for the annual 
payments on the said sum after crediting annual net revenues 
from the operation of the said system. 

The debentures are to be dated as may be appropriate 
having regard to the date of the borrowing and are to be pay¬ 
able in Twenty (20) annual instalments of' principal In each 
of the years 1961 to 1980, inclusive, with interest at a rate not 
exceeding Seven (1%) per cent per annum. 

NOTICE 

The above is a true synopsis of a proposed by-law of the 
Village of Lomond which has been introduced and which will 
be finally passed by the Council within four weeks from the 
assent of the proprietary electors thereto. 

Monday, the 15th day of February, 1960, at The Village 
Office, in the Village of Lomond, has been fixed by the Council 
as the time and place for taking the vote of the proprietary 
electors thereon and the poll will be open for such purpose 
between the hours of 10:00 o’clock in the forenoon and 7:00 
o'clock in the afternoon. 

DATED at the Village of Lomond, in the Province of 
Alberta, this 15th day of January, 1960.

lower gasoline prices 


The new DC8 jets are powered by a special jet fuel, as carefully tailored 
for its job as the gasoline for your car. 

Today gasolines and jet fuels are only two types of a whole range of 
products made by Imperial from petroleum. These products—over 600 
of them—cover everything: gases which are turned into plastics, waxes 
for all kinds of needs, greases, lubricating oils, fuel oils and asphalts. 

This efficiency in getting everything out of every gallon of crude oil is an 
important reason for lower fuel prices—whether for a DC8, a jalopy or the

VILLAGE OF LOMOND 

SYNOPSIS OF BY-LAW NO. 160 

This By-law provides for the issurance of debentures of 
the Village in the sum of $38,800.00, for the purpose of provid¬ 
ing a sewer and sewage disposal system for the Village. 

The By-law authorizes the Council to enter into con¬ 
tracts for the installation of the said system, and for such pur¬ 
pose to issue debentures of the Village in the sum of $38,800.00. 

The sum of $28,997.00 is to be charged by way of special 
assessment for local improvements against all properties 
abutting the streets or avenues wherein the said sewer system 
is to be installed, payable at an annual rate per front foot of 
frontage of 30 cents, payable for a period of Twenty (20) 
years. The balance is borrowed on-the credit of the Village at 
large and provision is made for the levying of annual rates on 
ail taxable property in the Village to provide for the annual 
payments on the said sum after crediting annual net revenues 
from the operation of the said system. 

The debentures are to be dated as may be appropriate 
having regard to the date of the borrowing and are to be pay¬ 
able In Twenty (20) annual instalments of principal in each 
of the years 1961 to 1980, inclusive, with interest at a rate not 
exceeding Seven (7%) per cent per annum. 

NOTICE 

The above is a true synopsis of a. proposed by-law of the 
Village of Lomond which has been introduced and which will 
be finally passed by the Council within tour weeks from the 
assent of the proprietary electors thereto. 

Monday, the 15th day of February, 1960, at The Village 
Office, in the Village of Lomond, has been fixed by the Council 
as the time and place for taking the vote of the proprietary 
electors thereon and the poll will be open for such purpose 
between the hours of 10:00 o’clock in the forenoon and 7:00 
o’clock in the afternoon. 

DATED at the Village of Lomond, in the Province of 
Alberta, this 15th day of January, 1960.
