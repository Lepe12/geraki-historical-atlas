Road Travel Ban 
On Purple Gas 


Action which seems strangely lack¬ 
ing in any common sense, was taken 
against Dinton Sammons of Gleichen 
for moving a combine over the road 
with use of purple gas. His tractor 
was impounded for two weeks. His 
offence was that he went out on the 
road with purple gas in his tractor, 
while moving the combine from one 
farm to another in the course of his 
farming operations. The purple gas 
was already in the tractor, so probab¬ 
ly unthinkingly he just steamed ahead 
But an inspector of the dep’t. of the 
provincial secretary stopped him and 
to his horror discovered the purple 
gas. 

So Sammons was up before Magis¬ 
trate Fitch of Calgary, fined $1 and 
icosts and his tractor impounded for 
14 days. This may throw out tw-o 
weeks of combining for the farmer. 
Probably many fields and farmers 
were depending on this combine. Per¬ 
haps the sawfly can complete destruc¬ 
tion on fields which might have been 
saved if he had had use of that trac¬ 
tor. 

It does not seem to add up to any¬ 
thing satisfactory, nor does it seem a 
violation of the law. The tractor was 
being used in farm operation, even 
though that operation took it out on 
the road to get from here to there. 

However, farmers with property 
separated by a strip of road, had bet¬ 
ter watch out. An inspector lurks in 
every bush, and to hoot with getting 
the crop off. 


War Notes 


Yields Vary Greatly But 
12 Hush. Considered Average 
Shortage of l-’arm Labor 

Harvesting is in full swing in this 
district and the estimated average 
yield is about 12 bushels per acre, just 
about half of last year’s average yield 
of 22 bushels . 

Following a very dry summer, witn 
such rainfall as was rceeived being in 
the form of local showers, great vari¬ 
ation is shown in the yields in various 
parts of the .district. Some stubbled- 
in fields are not worth the cost of 
harvesting, while, generally speaking 
crops sown on summer-fallowed land 
are turning out better than was anti¬ 
cipated. Where heavy showers were 
received in July, yields of 20 and 25 
bushels per acre are reported, but 
these are the more favored districts. 
The wheat is grading fairly good, 
mostly No. 1 and No. 2 Northern. 

This harvest is one of the earliest 
experienced in many years. The ef¬ 
fect of war is being felt, however, in 
the shortage of harvest labor, and 
hoys of 14 and 15 years of age are be¬ 
ing used as tractor drivers. The 
greater part of-the harvest this yem 
is being done by the straight combine 
method.

Motor Age -— Motor Roads 


^HE Alberta Motor Association is calling for sup T 
port in its efforts to persuade the government to 
spend all monies collected by way of the motor industry 
on highways. It asks the support of all farm organiza¬ 
tions, no less than urban, because better roads are act¬ 
ually of more concern to the farm motorist than to those 
in cities and town. 

A federal survey in United States on the question of 
road building reached one definite conclusion, namely 
that the motorist should not be expected to pay the fut). 
share of road costs, because good roads have a gen eral 
social and economic value in which others besides mo¬ 
torists share. There was difference of opinion on the 
proportion of ccsts which should be carried by way of 
gas tax levy and licenses. Some thought that the gas 
and license taxes should bear 76%- of the tost of road 
building, the government putting in the other 2S%..£Hh- 
ers considered that no more than 45% should be expect¬ 
ed of car users. ** 

In any event it was agreed that there should be a pro¬ 
portion of the cost of all road expenditure carried by the 
general revenue of the state. The view was taken 
that good highways are a major source -of revenue to 
the people in general through encouragement to travel, 
and should be a major feature of attention and expendi¬ 
ture. 

This province falls lamentably short of this finding. 
Motorists on provincial highways have more than paid 
for all road building as would be shown clearly if the 
motor revenues had been separated from general reven¬ 
ues. So far from having any general gov’t assistance in 
paying-for roads, the gas and motor taxes have bolster¬ 
ed up the general revenue, for the promotion of other 
enterprises of government which will never -bring the 
general benefits that a good road program would bring. 

Basing this year’s returns on the experience of past 
years the revenue paid by motorists would be round $6,- 
000,000. The gas restrictions will naturally affect the 
figure, but a big credit is due from past years. If the 
government added 26%' to this, it would make $8,000,000 
available for road building and maintenance. But there 
is to be less than $3,000,000 expended on provincial 
roads this year. So motorists pay in twice as much tax

Youth Training Plan 

0 NE constructive effort born of depression years 
was the federal-provincial Youth Training Scheme 
which absorbed a number of unemployed youth of both 
sexes in various types of training, the better to fit them 
for wage-earning opportunities. 

The object and principles of the scheme found gen¬ 
eral support, and it is believed that to a limited degree, 
it was successful in training young people and bring¬ 
ing employment. Under the relatively small sum avail-* 
able for the purpose, no great proportion of unemployed 
youth was served however. 

But this scheme, happily launched without any 
thought of war and purely as an encouragement to 
young people, has received new impetus under war de¬ 
mands. The ground work laid in pre-war years has been 
adapted to serve war needs. This year there will be 
4500 Alberta graduates under the youth training scheme 
and the instruction which they have received, will make 
them of immediate use to the nation. One branch gives 
training for war industries, another trains enlisted men, 
and a third gives pre-enlistment training for ground 
crews for the airforce. There are 7 schools in Alberta 
with full facilities for training. The schools have s|h*c- 
ialized in making of engine lathes, ami according i,> the 
Dominion-supervisor, Alberta is leading the Dominion 
in this type of output. 

Dominion records show that the great majority of 
these young people, upon graduation, have been at once 
absorbed into industry or have enlisted in some special 

branch of the service. 

The federal-provincial youth training scheme has the 
advantage of having been established befo.e the war. 
and has been able to focus on war requirements without 
much difficulty. It is to be hoped that provision is now¬ 
being made to continue and broaden the scheme so that 
it may play an important part in the post war years.
