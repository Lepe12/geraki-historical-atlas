I was one of the lucky people to get into the IMAX screening 
of Megalopolis on its second day at TIFE What I can say is, 
everything you heard about Megalopolis is true. It feels like it 
was made in a parallel universe. Switches from film noir 
aesthetics to Ancient Rome period piece vibes while the 
characters’ mannerisms are Shakespearean and the exteriors 
look both contemporary and futuristic at the same time. The 
sheer base of familiarity we get from any movie — a plot — 
seems absent for certain periods and we only get these 
Verhoeven-esque, over-the-top satiric sequences mish-mashed 
together. Everything is really artificial yet likely deliberately 
so, as it is as the subtitle suggests, “a fable”. Then halfway 
through, a guy dressed as a reporter enters the theatre in real 
life and starts interviewing Adam Driver’s character in the 
film. Before the confusion diffuses, we get hit by a split-screen 
montage separated to 3 panels, and things still get crazier and 
trippier after that. For the whole runtime, Coppola is Review

TIFF platforms art that is difficult to platform. Two features and one shorts collection from Palestinian 
artists premiered at the festival: From Ground Zero, No Other Land, and To a Land Unknown. Sudan, 
remember us is a document of the doomed 2019 revolution. They are powerful works uplifting and 
immortalizing the disempowered in the SWANA region. Ukrainian film was also featured heavily: 
documentaries U Are the Universe and Viktor, and Polish drama Under the Volcano. But TIFF didn’t come 
out unscathed. Platforming “both sides” invites derision from “both sides”. At the Opening Night 
screening of the world premiere of Nutcrackers, anti-RBC, pro-climate, pro-Palestine protesters disrupted 
opening remarks from TIFF CEO Cameron Bailey. Protesters would also criticize the screening of 
Israel-funded film Bliss (Hemda). In the latter half of the festival, Russians at War, a documentary 
co-produced by Canada & France, was criticized as “pro-Russian propaganda’, inciting protests in front of 


TIFF venues and comments from Deputy PM Chrystia Freeland and Ukrainian diplomat Oleh Nikolenko. 


In a constructive spirit, I will mention the 17th annual Toronto Palestine Film Festival is beginning 
September 25th. They collaborated with other groups to form a counter-programming initiative here at 
Innis Town Hall Sept 5 - 8. The Toronto Ukrainian Film Festival comes back for its 3rd year in April 2025.
