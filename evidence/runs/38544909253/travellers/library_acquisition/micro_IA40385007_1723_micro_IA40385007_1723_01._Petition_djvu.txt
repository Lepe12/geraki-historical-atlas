Questions Presented for Review. 

1. Whether the President has statutory or inherent con- 
stitutional authority to settle legally enforceable claims of 
American citizens against foreign states, agencies, and con- 
trolled entities pending in United States courts and nullify 
judgments of United States courts adjudicating such claims? 

2. Whether the President has statutory or inherent con- 
stitutional authority to nullify attachment liens and judgment 
liens imposed by process of United States courts on property 
of foreign states, agencies, and controlled entities to secure 
or satisfy legally enforceable claims and judgments of 
American citizens and to order the transfer of such property 
out of the reach of process of United States courts? 


3. Whether Presidential action substantially diminish- 
ing or destroying the value of legally enforceable claims of 
American citizens against foreign states, agencies, and con- 
trolled entities is an unconstitutional taking of property?

On April 28, 1981, Petitioner filed the Action Below, in 
the United States District Court for the Central District of 
California, for injunctive and declaratory relief against The 
Secretary of the Treasury of the United States and The 
United States (hereinafter jointly referred to as the ‘‘Gov- 
ernment’’), seeking (1) a preliminary and permanent in- 
junction enjoining the Government from interference with 
proceedings in, or related to, an action by Petitioner in the 
same court known as Dames & Moore v. Atomic Energy 
Organization of Iran, United States District Court for the 
Central District of California, No. CV 79-04918 LEW (Px) 
(hereinafter the ‘‘Underlying Action’’), and (2) a de- 
claratory judgment that the so-called ‘‘Algerian Declara-

Jurisdiction was invoked under 28 U.S.C. Sections 1331,

As set forth in the following section of this Petition, the 
issues raised here involve novel, fundamental questions of 
law going to the core of the government that our Constitution 
creates — its allocation and separation of powers among 
the executive, legislative, and judicial branches. Billions 
of dollars, as well as delicate constitutional principles, are 
at stake. This is a case of imperative public importance. 

If the Court is to accept an Iranian case for review, it 
must do so now, without awaiting a judgment from the 
Court of Appeals. For, in the absence of this Court’s ac- 
cepting an Iranian case for review, the Government will 
require all persons holding Iranian funds and securities to 
transfer those funds and securities to the Federal Reserve 
Bank in New York by 12:00 noon on June 19, 1981 under 
pain of criminal penalties. Moreover, unless the Court ac- 
cepts an Iranian case for review, the Government apparently 
intends to transfer all Iranian funds and securities out of the 
country, and out of the reach of United States court process 
entirely, by July 19, 1981, thus rendering major issues pre- 
sented in the Iranian cases academic, if not moot.’
