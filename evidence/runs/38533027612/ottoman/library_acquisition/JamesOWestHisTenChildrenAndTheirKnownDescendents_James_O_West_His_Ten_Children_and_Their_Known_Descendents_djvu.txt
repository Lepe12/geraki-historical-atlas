The numbering system: No number has been given to James 0. West, 
the earliest known ancestor. Each of his children is numbered in 
order of birth. 
Example : 

1. Mathfew A. West, first child of James 0. West 

2. James M. West, second child of James 0. West 
1.1 First child of first child of James 0. West 
2.1 First child of second child of James 0. West 



1. 



JAMES 0. WEST was born 18 December 1808 in Kentucky. This statement is 
based on the 1850 Pike County, Illinois census and his gravestone inscription. 
Later when his children were asked during census years where their father was 
born two responded Tennessee and three said Alabama. None said Kentucky. 

He married Jane Johnson. She was born ca 1814-17 in Kentucky. 

Their first child was born in Kentucky in 1832, the second in Illinois in 
1835, the third in Missouri in 1837 and the fourth in Woodford County, Illinois 
in 1838. In the 1840 census they lived in White County, Illinois. So in 
eight years they moved from Kentucky to Illinois, then to Missouri, back to the 
north central area of Illinois and then south almost to the Kentucky border. 
Then west again in the next ten years to Pike County, Illinois which is on the 
Missouri border. This migration pattern is based on the known birth places of 
the children and the census. 

On the 28th of September 1850 James 0. West purchased for $55.00 forty-four 
and 31/100 acres of land in Pike County, Illinois. 

He died 23 February 1851, aged 42 years, 2 months, 5 days and buried in the 
Starkey Cemetery. This cemetery is located about 10 miles northwest of New 
Salem, Pike County, Illinois. Muriel and Oscar Gregory visited this cemetery 
in 1985. It was so overgrown they could not penetrate it beyond a few yards. 

Jane West and her children moved to Scott County, Illinois between 1856 (when 
Anna Jane West married in Pike County) and 1858 when Jane asked for a partition 
of the Pike County land. 

When James 0. West died all the children were minors and it appears from 
court records in Pike County that nothing was done about the land until about 
1858 when Mathew, James M. and Anna Jane were of legal age. Then Jane West 
in the March Term of 1858 asked the court for a partition of the land subject 
to her dower and life right to one-third of the land. The commissioners 
appointed to divide and allot the land reported that it was unimproved, of 
inferior quality and could not be divided without injury to the estate. The 
court then ordered the land sold at public sale at the Pittsfield courthouse 
and that Jane West be allowed her dower rights from the proceeds. Each child 
was named for a share. 

This sale evidently did not take place. On 18 June 1870 in consideration of 
$300.00 James M. West received a quit-claim deed to the land from his brothers 
and sisters. The mother, Jane West, was not mentioned. A sister, Sarah 
Elizabeth West, did not sign the deed. We can assume they were deceased. 

In the 1860 Scott County, Illinois census Jane West lived near Glasgow, 
Scott County, Illinois - owning land valued at $1250.00. She was a weaver, 
the four youngest children were with her. She had given her age in 1850 as 
36, in 1860 she was 43! Both census showed her birth place as Kentucky and 
in later census all the children agreed on this. I have found no deeds showing 
she or her family bought or sold land in Scott County. 



Jane West died before June 1870, place of burial unknown. She and James 0. 
West were the parents of 10 known children, seven sons and three daughters. 
Four of their sons served in the Civil War. Probably a fifth son served but 
no record has been found. 

There was probably a West family bible. On 18 June 1910 William E. West 
Page 37) made the following affidavit in his Civil War pension papers: 
"The last time I saw the family bible sometime about 1888 it was in Scott 
County, Illinois. My brother had it and he took it to Bloomington, Illinois. 
His health was bad and he moved to Kansas with his wife. He died there and 
also his wife died later. I have no idea what became of it nor where it is." 
Whether the bible is in existence today is unknown.

Mathew Aaron West was born 1 November 1832 in Kentucky. 



He was with his parents in the 1850 Pike County, Illinois census (age 15). 
In the 1860 census he was a farm laborer, age 24, living with a family in 
Winchester, Scott County, Illinois. 

He enlisted 9 August 1862 in Co. H, 129 111. Inf. at Winchester. Mustered 
into service on 8 September 1862 at Pontiac, Illinois. He was a private. 
His description at that time: 29 years old, 5' 3", sandy hair, grey eyes, 
fair complexion, single, farmer. His company was mustered out 8 June 1865 
near Washington D.C. and he was discharged at Chicago, Illinois 22 June 1865. 

In the 1870 census he was a farm laborer, age 36, living with a family in 
Winchester, Scott County, Illinois. 

He married Elizabeth M. Baker 6 November 1871 at Winchester. She died 
17 November 1876. They had no children. 

In the 1880 census he was living in Pike County, Illinois with his sister, 
Anna Jane (West) Morgan. He was 48, single, laborer. 

On 29 December 1884 while living at Valley City, Pike County, Illinois 
he applied for a Civil War pension for service connected disability. While 
in service at Atlanta, Georgia on 22 June 1864 he was struck by a minnie ball 
in the right thigh and also received an injury in the back while making 
breast works in the state of Georgia - by lifting with a hand spike which 
slipped and wrenched his back. His claim was rejected. 

On 19 July 1890 he again made declaration for a pension. He was living in 
Scott County, Illinois, age 57. This time he was more specific - wounded 
right thigh in 1864 at Peach Tree Creek, Georgia; injured in back in 1864 
at Dallas Woods, Georgia. Military records showed him admitted to hospital 
at Savannah, Georgia 28 January 1865 with incised wound of knee by axe, 
returning to duty 10 March 1865. Pension approved 19 February 1891, 
commencing 28 July 1890 at $10. per month. 

In the 1900 census he was living in Greene County, Illinois with Robert 
and Minnie Rayborn. Minnie Rayborn was the daughter of John T. West, 
brother of Mathew. 

On 2 May 1894 he filed an application for increase of pension. He was 

living at Barrow, Greene County, Illinois. An examination indicated premature 

old age and disabilities arising therefrom - increase rejected. 

Mathew A. West died 21 October 1902, Scott County, Illinois. Buried at 
Bower Cemetery, Scott County.

James M. West was born 26 December 1835 in Carmi, White County, Illinois. 
This statement is based on information (probably given by James M. West) 
on his biographical card in the archives of the Central Illinois Conference 
of the Methodist Church. His widow, in his newspaper obituary, said he was 
born in Pike County, Illinois. An article on him in the McLean County, 
Illinois History (1908) said he was born in Scott County, Illinois. 

At the age of 14 (1850) he joined the Methodist Church in Pike County, 
Illinois. At the age of 21 (1856) he entered the ministry of the Methodist 
Church and served for thirty-eight years as an itinerant preacher. In 1858 
he joined the Illinois Conference. It was said that he was gifted in prayer 
and powerful in appeal to the unsaved. 

During those 38 years he served in 21 churches in, at least, 12 counties in 
central Illinois. At this time no Methodist minister was permitted to 
remain in the same station for more than two years. In 1862 and 1864 he 
did not have a church which may mean he served in the Civil War but no 
record of service has been found. 

In the 1860 census he boarded with a family in Bath, Mason County, Illinois. 
He was 24, a Methodist Episcopal clergyman. 

He. married 12 April 1864 in Quincy, Adams County, Illinois Mrs. Rachel 
(Henry) Salthouse. She was born 16 December 1833 in Derry County, Ireland. 
She was a widow with four children, Emma, Ellen, John and Henry. James, 
Rachel and the children lived in Moweaqua, Shelby County, Illinois in 1870. 

On 18 June 1870 James M. West bought from his brothers and sisters the 
forty-four acres of land that his father had purchased in Pike County in 
1850 for $55.00. James paid $300. to be divided between the other eight 
heirs. This deed showed all parties were of Scott County, Illinois. On 
9 July 1870 James and Rachel West sold this land for $400. They were of 
Shelby County, Illinois. 



In 1880 James and Rachel and son, James A., lived in Elkhart, Logan County, 
Illinois. Because of ill health James withdrew from active service in the 
ministry in 1894 and lived in Bloomington, McLean County, Illinois from that 
time until 1906. 

In September or October of 1906 James and Rachel went to McPherson, Kansas 
to spend the winter, hoping the change of climate would be beneficial to 
his health. For several years he had been suffering with pernicious anaemia. 
He died 8 November 1906 at the home of his son-in-law, Dr. R. S. Simpson, in 
McPherson, Kansas . 

Rachel West died 11 January 1910. Buried beside her husband in McPherson, 
Kansas. She was survived by three children of her first marriage: John T. 
Salthouse, Dr. Henry L. Salthouse, Mrs. R. S. Simpson - all of McPherson. 
A daughter, Mrs. D. H. Henschie, of Pawnee, Illinois predeceased her mother 
by a few months. She was survived by a son of her second marriage: James A. 
West, Illinois.

2.11 Dorothy Irene West was born 14 January 1905 in Bloomington, Illinois. 
By skipping fifth grade and by getting extra credits for private 
violin lessons she finished high school in three years and enrolled 
• in the University of Illinois at the age of 16. After four years in 
the College of Music she found she would not make a living with her 
violin and changed to Education, graduating in 1926. She taught English 
for one year at Havre, Montana and then returned to the University of 
Illinois where she was a part time teacher and student. She received 
her PHD degree in 1938. 



9. 



Dorothy West was teaching English at McKendree College, Lebanon, Illinois 
when she met and married a widower, Reinhold Barrett Hohn. He was a Swabian, 
born in the Black Forest area of Germany in 1888. He was a professor of 
Educational Psychology. 

Dorothy and Reinhold bought land on a hill, high above the Lake of the Ozarks, 
where they lived in a cabin built from slabs left from logging. They bought 
a sawmill and, from standing timber on their property, built a home and three 
poultry houses - each of the latter housing 1500 chicks. For a more colorful 
and detailed description - see a feature article in the December 14, 1947 
issue of the St. Louis Post-Dispatch. This property is now a camp for the 
St. Louis Boy Scouts. 

Dorothy died 17 May 1959, Reinhold in 1963. Both are buried in the Ozark 
Methodist Chapel Cemetery south of Gravois Mills, Missouri.

in 1931 and worked for about a year at Allen-Bradley Company of Milwaukee. 
He returned to the University and received a Masters in the science of Physics 
and then transferred to Business Administration where he taught part time 
and took graduate courses. 

In 1937 he went to Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania to work at Westinghouse Electric 
Company. He finished his work on a PHD degree at the University of Pittsburgh. 
In 1942 he moved to Milwaukee, Wisconsin to work at Harnischf eger Corp. where 
he advanced to Market Analyst. 

Robert met Mary Janet Cook at the University of Illinois. They were married 
25 June 1938 at Somonauk, Illinois. She was born 20 December 1916, Somonauk, 
Illinois, the daughter of Roy William Cook and Lila Jane Stratton. Mr. Cook 
was a country lawyer and later Mayor of Somonauk. Mary Janet died 1 November 
1972 and is buried in Oak Ridge Cemetery, Sanwich, Illinois. 

On a trip to Acapulco, Mexico Robert met Elaine Johanne (Schubring) Hercher. 
They were married 25 August 1973, Milwaukee, Wisconsin. She was born 
11 February 1910, Milwaukee, Wisconsin, the daughter of William A. Schubring 
and Anna Marie Wilde. Robert and Elaine live in Milwaukee.

Anna Jane West was born 8 April 1837 in Missouri. All the census records 
consistently show she was born in Missouri, although her death certificate 
and obituary say Ohio - two records she had no control over! 

She was with her parents in the 1850 Pike County, Illinois census. She 
was shown as Alvira J., age 13. This name caused much additional research 
before Muriel Gregory found the Pike County probate records which proved 
Anna Jane did belong to the James 0. West family. 

She united with the Methodist Church in Valley City, Illinois in 1853. She 
married (1) John P. Morgan 25 January 1856 in Pike County, Illinois. He 
was born 1830 in Kentucky, the son of Charles Morgan and Mary Ham. 

In 1860 the family was living in Griggsville, Pike County, Illinois. In 
1870 they were in Oxville, Scott County, Illinois. John P. Mcrgan was a 
farmer. He died 8 October 1875. 

Anna Jane was left with 9 children ranging in age from four months to 
nineteen years. In 1880 she was in Pike County, Illinois with three of 
her daughters living with her, also her brother, Mathew A. West. Four of 
the children were married, one was evidently dead, two sons were living 
with married siblings. 

She also had a widowed man in the 1880 household as a boarder. One wonders 
how she managed to rear her young children and she probably did so by taking 
in boarders. 

She married (2) William Parker 12 May 1891 in Vandalia, Audrain County, 
Missouri. In 1900 they were living in Collinsville, Madison County, Illinois. 
They rented their home, he was a church janitor. He died 27 November 1900. 

I have been unable, to date, to find Anna Jane in a 1910 census. She entered 
the Jacksonville State Hospital, Morgan County, Illinois 26 January 1912 
where she remained for 8 years until her death on 11 December 1920. Funeral 
services were held at the home of T. F ; Napier in Griggsville, Illinois 
and she was buried in the Griggsville Cemetery. T. F. Napier was the husband 
of her daughter, Sarah Caroline. 

Although she signed a marriage consent "Jane Morgan" - it is believed she 
was called "Annie". She was mentioned thus in the Gregory bible. In 1900 
she said she had had 11 children, 8 living. We know of only 10 children, 
another child probably died young.

3.4 Margaret E. ( Maggie ) Morgan was born 16 November 1861 at Valley City, 

Pike County, Illinois. She grew up on a farm and went to a country school. 

She married, at 17, on 21 July 1879 Edwin Ruth Vanhyning at Pittsfield, 
Pike County, Illinois. He was born 10 June 1854 in Illinois, the son of 
Wells Vanhyning and Matilda Fitzsimmons . 

Margaret was slender and rather tall, with light brown hair. Her husband 
was a small man, shorter than his wife, with dark hair and a moustache. 

Edwin worked as a farm laborer. His health was poor, he suffered from 
asthma. He died 28 May 1915 at Bluffs, Illinois from general disability. 
He was 60 years old. 

After her husband's death Margaret hired out at harvest time to cook for 
threshers, often staying a week at a time. Mabel (Gregory) Christianson, 
a granddaughter, sometimes went along as her helper and vividly remembers 
peeling apples for half a dozen pies at a time. 

To eke out the family income Margaret made and sold quilts. Mabel 
Christianson remembers one in particular, made of white sateen with pink 
tulips. Margaret taught Mabel to tat and crochet and cook. Margaret was 
an excellent cook. Another granddaughter, Margaret (Gregory) Dunlap, recalls 
that Margaret was very resourceful, knowing and using edible wild plants. 
She had the ability to prepare a good nourishing meal when it seemed that 
there was little in the house from which a meal could be made. 

After her husband's death Margaret made a home for her youngest son, Clyde, 
first in Bluffs, later in Springfield. When he married she returned to Bluffs. 

In her later years she became almost blind from cateracts in both eyes and 
suffered from rheumatism as well as from a heart condition. She died in 
Bluffs 5 July 1934 of myocarditis, complicated by hot weather and rheumatism. 
She is buried in the Naples Cemetery beside her husband, a single headstone 
marking their graves. She was 72. 

Edwin and Margaret Vanhyning had nine children, six of whom lived to maturity. 

3.41 Elizabeth May ( Lizzie ) Vanhyning was born 3 May 1880 in Pike County, 
Illinois. She attended school in Naples, Scott County, Illinois. 
After graduating from high school, age 18, she married 19 October 1898 
in Bluffs, Illinois to Edward Newton Gregory. He was born 12 July 1876 
at Bloomfield, Scott county, Illinois, the son of William Edward Gregory 
and Amanda B. Campbell. 

Ed and Lizzie lived all their lives in Naples, where he was a

4. John Thomas West was born 13 October 1838 in Montroseville, Woodford 
County, Illinois. He was with his parents in the 1850 Pike County, 
Illinois census (age 12). I have not found him in 1860. 

He was mustered into service on 25 May 1861 in Co. K, 14th Reg., Illinois 
Vols, at Jacksonville, Illinois and mustered out with company on 24 June 
1864 at Springfield, Illinois. He reinlisted in Co., K, 8 Reg't., Illinois 
Infantry 3 October 1864 and mustered out 2 October 1865. He was a private. 
He was described as 5*2", auburn hair, light eyes and fair complexion. 

The Captain of Co. K stated that John T. West grew so fleshy that his 
comrades nicknamed him "Gunny" - they said he looked like a stuffed gunny 
sack. But during 1862 while the company was campaigning in Tennessee he 
fell sick and became greatly changed in appearance and never recovered his 
healthy robust appearance. He was sent to hospital from LaGrange or Boliver, 
Tennessee. 

Another fellow service man said that during January 1864 while they were 
in winter quarters in the rear of Vicksburg John T. West caught cold and was 
ill. And that he got very sick during the Maridian (Maradon) campaign and 
had to be hauled in ambulance and was not able for duty the rest of his term 
of service. 

In 1870 he and his brother, Charles, were living in Glasgow, Scott County, 
Illinois (age 30). He was a farmer and owned land valued at $1000.00. 

He married in the courthouse at Winchester, Scott County, Illinois on 
10 October 1871 Elizabeth Jane Vestal (by Robert Southwell, J.P.). They 
lived in Alsey, Scott County in 1880 (age 41). 

He died in Alsey 23 May 1888 of double pneumonia, after an illness of eleven 
days. He has a gravestone at the Bower Cemetery giving his age as 49 years, 
7 months, 10 days. 

His widow applied for a widow's Civil War pension 21 March 1889. Her claim 
was rejected - no proof that his death was caused by war service. 

Elizabeth West married Albert Crane in Scott County, Illinois 17 March 1890. 

On 17 November 1890 she again applied for a pension saying that she and her 
children were without means of support. Many people made affidavits to 
support her claim. Of genealogical interest were those by Charles G. West 
(brother of John T.) and Mary C. Bird (mother of Elizabeth) and Biddy M. 
West (sister-in-law of John T.) and Anna Bird (probably sister of Elizabeth). 
Many other surnames were connected to the West family - by census or by 
marriage. Her claim was again rejected. 

On October 31, 1894 John H. Pfenninger was appointed guardian of the West 
children under 16 and on 19 January 1895 filed a claim for them for their 
father's war service. One of the affidavits concerned the John T. West 
family bible. Martin Hester, a Notary Public, examined the bible in March 
1901. "Poorly cared for as both covers and the leaves showing date of 
publication are entirely gone but the record of five children in a crude 
form seems perfect. The bible is one of antiquity."

William E. West was born 2 June 1840 in White County, Illinois. 

He was with his parents in the 1850 Pike County, Illinois census (age 10). 

He lived at Glasgow, Scott County, Illinois when he enlisted in the 

Union army at Jacksonville, Illinois on 25 May 1861 in Co. C, 14th 

111. Inf. He was 21. Discharged at Springfield, Illinois 18 June 1864. 

Enrolled 3 March 1865 as a private in Co. E, 4 U.S. Veterans Volunteers 

Infantry. Honorably discharged at Columbus, Ohio on 3 March 1866. 

His description at this time: 5' 7", light complexion, blue or grey eyes, 

light or sandy hair. He was a farmer. 

He married 4 January 1869 in Scott county, Illinois Mariah Truesdale. 
They lived near Glasgow in 1870. Her father, John Truesdale, lived with 
them and owned the land. John Truesdale died in 1871, Mariah West was 
one of his heirs. 

In a search for West family land transactions in Scott County - I found 
grantor and grantee deeds by William E. West only. In July 1866 he 
bought 40 acres and in March 1867 sold same. In 1868 he sold 80 acres. 
In 1872 he sold 39 acres. 

October 22 1867 William and Maria West sold 40 acres of land to his brother, 
John T. West, for $500. 17 February 1873 they sold another parcel of land 
to John T. West. This land was in Scott County. 

About 1872 William and Maria moved to Palmyra, Macoupin County, Illinois, 
where they lived in the 1880 census. Maria died in Palmyra 11 January 1899. 

He applied for a Civil War pension 21 July 1890 because of weakness of 
shoulders and general debility. M Fever by exposure previous to the Battle 
of Shiloh 6 April 1862. Said soldier was sick when the battle commenced 
but got up out of bed and did duty through the two days fight. Scarcely 
able to walk. He was furloughed in July 1863 on account of sickness, 
returned to the command about September 1863. He was never considered a 
healthy man after the Battle of Shiloh, Tennessee." 

He was pensioned at $6. per month. He applied and received increases - 
in 1920 he was drawing $72. per month. At this time he was totally 
disabled - "Arterial Schleriosis, almost blind, partially deaf, mind wanders 
and he wanders away, especially at night. Could not dress or undress 
himself" . 

He married (2) Mrs. Mary A. Caldwell (nee Proso) in Palmyra, Illinois 
13 September 1904. She died in 1907. He married (3) Mary E. Burton in 
Palmyra, Illinois 21 September 1907. 

Sometime between 1907 and 1910 he moved to Alton, Madison County, Illinois. 
He died 20 October 1921 at Alton. 

In the 1870 and 1880 census there was a child living with William and 

his first wife. But in his pension papers he said he had never had children.

since the shoulder was lame and painful continually, causing about 1/4 
disability. He did not receive a pension. At this time he was described 
as 43 years old, 5 f 6", light complexion, dark hair, blue eyes, a farmer.

In 1972 John Napier, Griggsville, Illinois - a grandson of 
John P. Morgan - sent a list of the Charles Morgan children 
to his cousin, Ethel (Simpson) Puhlman. His mother, Sarah 
Caroline (Morgan) Napier, had written this list of the children 
with their birthdates. The birthdates, as given by her, are 
used in this record. Where obvious errors exist, I have so 
noted. Future researchers should be cautious about these 
dates .

49. 



CHARLES MORGAN was born 9 January 1793 in North Carolina. This 
statement is based on his gravestone inscription and the 1850 census. 

He married Mary Ham, born 17 March 1803 in South Carolina, the daughter 
of Joshua and Mary Ham*. Charles and Mary were probably married in 
Bedford County, Tennessee where her parents lived in 1820 and 1830 but 
no marriage records are available for that period. 

I believe they lived in Bedford County, Tennessee in the 1830 census - 
two dwellings from Joshua and Mary Ham. I have not found them in 1840. 
Sometime between 1840 and 1850 they moved to Pike County, Illinois where 
they lived in the 1850 census. They evidently followed her brother, 
James Ham, who emigrated there from Tennessee in the 1830' s. 

Charles Morgan died 15 December 1851, aged 58 years, 11 months, 6 days. 
He is buried in the Ham Cemetery, Pike County, Illinois. This cemetery 
is a 1/4 acre plot high on a bluff overlooking the Illinois River. It 
is north of Valley City and about two miles south of Chambersburg. There 
have been no burials since 1907. 

Mary Morgan was with her son, William, and his family in the 1860 Pike 
County, Illinois census. She died 11 March 1861, aged 57 years, 11 months, 
24 days. She is buried in the Ham Cemetery. 



* Joshua Ham was born 1770-75, place unknown. A son's biography in a 
Pike County, Illinois history says "Joshua witnessed the battle of 
Guilford Court House in 1781, though then a lad M . 

"He married Mary (Polly) Williams, daughter of General Williams of Rev. 
War fame." Joshua may have married in Pendleton County, South Carolina. 
His second son was born at Granville, Pendleton County (now Pickens) in 1796. 

On 26 March 1797 Joshua bought 69 acres in Washington District, Pendleton 
County - located on the north east side of Keowee River. In the 1800 
census they lived in Pendleton County in the part now Oconee County. On 
20 January 1810 Joshua and Polly Ham sold 150 acres of land situated on the 
east side of the Seneca River in Pendleton County. 

Joshua migrated to Tennessee about 1810 and was living in Bedford County 
in the 1820 and 1830 census. In 1822 he purchased 175 acres of land above 
Shelbyville - which he sold in 1827. 

"He died in McCracken County, Kentucky in 1837, his wife in 1839." 

Quotations are from "Old Settlers of Pike County, Illinois". I have not 
been able to verify these statements with authentic records. 



Children of Charles and Mary Morgan 



Joel Morgan was born 27 March 1825 in Tennessee. This birthdate 
may not be correct. His age in census records was not consistent. 
Joel is a Ham family name. He and his wife, Mary, appear in the 
1850 and 1860 Pike County, Illinois census. He was a cooper and a 
farmer. The five known children are taken from the 1860 census. 

1.1 James H. Morgan born ca 1851 in Illinois. 

1.2 William M. Morgan born ca 1853 in Illinois. 

1.3 Daughter (name illegible) born ca 1855 in Illinois. 

1.4 John Morgan born ca 1858 in Illinois. 

1.5 Sarah A. Morgan born ca 1860 in Illinois. 

Eliza Jane Morgan was born 28 August 1829 in Kentucky. In the 1850 
Pike County census she gave her age as 26 (1824). The birth date 
may not be correct. 

She married 23 April 1848 in Pike County, Illinois to Jacob Cheek. 
She died between 1850 and 1856. Jacob Cheek married Mrs. Rebecca P. 
Wing 8 June 1856 in Pike County. Eliza Jane and Jacob Cheek had two 
known children. 

2.1 Mary Cheek born ca 1849 in Illinois. On 1850 census with parents. 

2.2 Sarah Elizabeth Cheek was born August 1852 in Illinois. She lived 
with her uncle, William Morgan, and her grandmother, Mary (Ham) 
Morgan, in the 1860 Pike County census (age 7). In 1870 she was 

a servant in the Henry Oliver household in Griggsville, Illinois. 
She was 17. 

She married Jeremiah B. Thompson on 16 November 1873 (by Wm. J. 
Rutledge, Minister of Methodist Church) in Pike County Illinois . 
In 1880 the family lived in Perry Township, Pike County. In 1900 
they lived in Montezuma Townshio, Pike County. Both census showed 
she could not read or write. They had 5 children, 2 are known. 

2.21 James L. Thompson born October 1876 in Illinois. 

2.22 Mary B. Thompson born January 1888 in Illinois. 

John P. Morgan was born 1830 in Kentucky. He was not with his parents 
in the 1850 census - age 20. He married 25 January 1856 Anna Jane West 
in Pike County, Illinois. She was born 8 April 1837 in Missouri, the 
daughter of James 0. West and Jane Johnson. 

John was a farmer. They lived in Pike County, Illinois in 1860 and 
in Scott County, Illinois in 1870. He listed real estate valued at 
$1200. in the 1870 census but no land record has been found.

John P. Morgan died 8 October 1875, place unknown. The date is from 
the obituary of Anna Jane Morgan.

4. James Clayborne Morgan was born 25 May 1832 in Kentucky. Clayborne ' 
is a Ham family name. He was with his parents in the 1850 Pike County, 
Illinois census (age 18). He was a cooper. He married Adeline Bright 
19 July 1856 in Pike County. 

5. Tempy Ann Morgan was born 6 May 1835 in Kentucky. She was not with her 
parents in the 1850 census. She was listed as a daughter on the Napier 
list. She could have married young or died. 

6. William Morgan was born 16 April 1837 in Kentucky. He was with his 
parents in the 1850 census. Age 14. In the 1860 census he was living 
in Griggsville, Pike County, Illinois with his wife, Catherine, his 
mother, Mary (Ham) Morgan, and his niece, Elizabeth Cheek. 

7. Sarah Ann Morgan was born 9 March 1840 in Kentucky. She was with her 
parents in the 1850 census. Age 10. No other information on her.
